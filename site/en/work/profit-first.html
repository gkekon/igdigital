It didn’t need more sales.

THE CHALLENGE

Good revenue.
Thin margin.

The ads were producing sales. That alone wasn’t enough. Thin retail margin, frequent discounts, and pressure for even more revenue.

A product could show strong ROAS and still leave almost nothing after the discount and the advertising cost. The logic of growth had to change:

→from volume to commercial contribution
→from discounting to the right product mix
→from spend to efficiency
→from revenue-first to profit-first

THE STRONGEST RESULT
−22.7% spend.
+7.2% purchases.

That is the performance result. The commercial one was bigger: +11.3% revenue with −31.5% discount value.

We didn’t improve the ad account. We improved the way the e-shop generated revenue.

THE APPROACH

Three changes. One different logic of growth.

01

Profit before ROAS

Good ROAS doesn’t mean good business.

We tied decisions to product margin, discount depth, CPA and the commercial contribution of each category. The objective moved from maximize revenue to maximize profitable revenue.

02

Product mix before discount

Not every sale needs a discount.

We steered demand toward a healthier commercial base: products with better margin, audiences with stronger purchase intent, less dependence on price.

03

Efficiency before scale

First a better system. Then a bigger one.

On Meta we cut spend noticeably. At the same time purchases and ROAS rose while CPA fell. Scale isn’t spending more — it’s producing more from what you already spend.
